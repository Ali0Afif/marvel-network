Who Links to Whom —
and Why Are Those
Different People?

Spider-Man is referenced by 35% of all Marvel Wikipedia pages. He links back to 9 of them. The gap between those two numbers is the whole story.

01
303 nodes. 1,784 edges.
The frozen Week 1 snapshot and why you must add nodes before edges.

Every node is a character from Wikipedia's Category: Marvel Comics superheroes. Every directed edge is a Wikipedia link — page A links to page B. 303 heroes. 1,784 arrows.

The critical detail: add nodes before edges. Otherwise NetworkX silently drops the 17 heroes whose pages have no links at all. Adding the full node list first keeps them in the graph where they belong.

import networkx as nx, pandas as pd

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#")
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    header=None, names=["source", "target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes["node_id"])   # isolates survive
G.add_edges_from(zip(edges["source"], edges["target"]))
# → 303 nodes · 1784 directed edges

Collapse to undirected: 1,784 arrows become 1,434 pairs — 350 edges point both ways. Average degree 2 × 1434 / 303 ≈ 9.5. Density: 3%. 97% of possible connections don't exist — a typical real network.

02
The degree distribution.
Toggle between in/out degree and linear/log axes below.

A node's degree is how many edges touch it. The degree distribution is the full portrait of the network: for each value k, how many nodes have exactly k connections? On linear axes the shape looks unremarkable — a pile near zero with a flat tail. Switch to log–log and the structure becomes unmissable.

Degree
Scale

The in-degree tail reaches Spider-Man at k = 106. The out-degree tail dies at k = 28 (Betsy Braddock). Same network, two very different shapes — which is the point of the next chapter.

03
In vs out: two different people.
Prominence and connectivity select for completely different things.
106 Spider-Man's in-degree. 106 of 302 possible heroes reference him on Wikipedia — 35% of the entire network — referencing one node. His out-degree: 9.

An in-link is conferred: some other article's editors decided this character was worth mentioning. An out-link is authored: someone sat down and wrote it into this page. One has no ceiling. The other has a budget. Two different generating mechanisms, two different distributions.

Spider-Man doesn't just have high centrality — he's become a reference point. Editors invoke him as context for characters who never met him. The network's "you are here" sign.

What surprised us

The gap between Spider-Man and everyone else is larger than we expected. In-degree 106 vs Hulk's 64 — that's not a small lead, it's a different category of node. Spider-Man has become a unit of measurement in how Wikipedia editors contextualize other heroes.

We also didn't expect 17 true isolates. Listing them out, several have decades of publication history. It's a reminder that the network reflects how Wikipedia was written, not the Marvel universe itself — and that gap matters every time we interpret a result from this dataset.
