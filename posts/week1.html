Bootstrap: First Look at the Marvel Graph

September 2026 · degree distributions · giant component · the islands

← All posts

What we asked

We started with a simple question: what does the Marvel Wikipedia network actually look like? 303 superheroes, edges drawn wherever one hero's Wikipedia page links to another's. Before doing anything fancy, we wanted ground truth on the shape of the thing — degree distributions, in vs out, the giant component, and whatever was left outside it.

Loading the data

The course provides a frozen snapshot: week1_edges.tsv (1784 directed edges) and week1_nodes.tsv (all 303 characters, including isolated ones). The key move is adding nodes before edges — otherwise the 17 isolated heroes vanish silently. 

import networkx as nx
import pandas as pd

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#")
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    header=None, names=["source", "target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes["node_id"])   # add ALL nodes first
G.add_edges_from(zip(edges["source"], edges["target"]))

# 303 nodes, 1784 edges, 17 isolates
print(G.number_of_nodes(), G.number_of_edges())

Degree distributions — linear

Most characters have very low degree. A handful have enormous in-degree. On a linear scale the distribution looks like a wall hugging the left axis with a long flat tail — almost all the mass is in the 0–10 range.

Degree distributions — log–log

Switch to log–log and the structure becomes clear: a heavy-tailed distribution consistent with a scale-free network. A few hubs pull in a disproportionate share of links. The out-degree tail is softer — outgoing links are more evenly spread.

Who links to whom — and why are those different people?

High in-degree means everyone else's page mentions you. High out-degree means your page mentions everyone else. These are very different characters:

The left column — the cultural pillars. Characters so foundational that half the universe's pages mention them in passing. Spider-Man's in-degree is 106 out of 302 possible: over a third of all heroes reference him. His out-degree is only 9. He's a landmark, not a connector.

The right column is different. Betsy Braddock (Psylocke) links out to 28 others but only 7 link back. Her page catalogs decades of team affiliations and retcons. Adam Warlock, Cloak and Dagger — characters with complex histories whose Wikipedia pages are essentially annotated relationship maps. They reach out; the network doesn't always reach back.

The asymmetry in one sentence: prominence (in-degree) and connectivity (out-degree) select for completely different things — cultural weight vs. narrative complexity.

The network

The giant component — and the islands

277 of 303 heroes sit in one giant connected component. Outside it: 18 smaller components — one cluster of 9, and 17 true isolates.

The isolates are a fascinating list:

  • Baymax, Super Rabbit, Breeze Barton — obscure or extremely old characters whose pages exist but were written in isolation from the main Wikipedia link ecosystem.
  • Miracleman — complex copyright history; his page has been carefully kept separate from the mainstream Marvel continuity on Wikipedia.
  • Yo-Yo Rodriguez, Captain Midlands — relatively recent or niche characters whose pages haven't been cross-linked yet.

The cluster of 9 is also worth looking at — a small connected sub-graph floating outside the main network. These are characters who reference each other but not the broader universe, forming their own little pocket continuity in Wikipedia's edit history.

What surprised us

The gap between Spider-Man and everyone else. In-degree 106 vs Hulk's 64 — that's not a small lead, that's a different category of node. Spider-Man doesn't just have high centrality; he's become a reference point in how Wikipedia editors contextualize other heroes. His page is the "you are here" marker of the Marvel universe.

We also didn't expect 17 true isolates — characters with no Wikipedia cross-links whatsoever. It's not that they're unpopular; several have real publication histories. It's a reminder that the network reflects how Wikipedia was written, not just what the characters did.
