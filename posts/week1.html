Who Links to Whom —
and Why Are Those
Different People?

Spider-Man is referenced by 35% of all Marvel Wikipedia pages. He links back to 9 of them. The gap between those two numbers is the entire story of this week.

01
303 nodes. 1,784 edges. Where they came from.
The frozen Week 1 snapshot from the course data page.

Every node is a character listed in Wikipedia's Category: Marvel Comics superheroes. Every directed edge is a Wikipedia link — page A links to page B — recorded when the snapshot was taken. 303 heroes. 1,784 arrows.

The most important thing when loading the data: add nodes before edges. Not because NetworkX will complain, but because it will silently drop the 17 characters whose pages link to nobody and nobody links to. Adding them explicitly first keeps them in the graph where they belong. 

import networkx as nx, pandas as pd

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#")
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    header=None, names=["source", "target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes["node_id"])   # isolates survive
G.add_edges_from(zip(edges["source"], edges["target"]))

print(G.number_of_nodes(), G.number_of_edges())
# → 303  1784

Make the graph undirected (ask only which pairs are connected at all) and the 1,784 arrows collapse to 1,434 pairs — 350 edges point both ways. Average undirected degree: 2 × 1434 / 303 ≈ 9.5. Density: 3%. A typical real network. 97% of the connections that could exist, don't.

02
The shape of the network.
Linear axes hide what log–log axes reveal.

Plot how many heroes have each in-degree — how many Wikipedia pages reference them. On linear axes it looks like a wall: everyone clusters near zero, a few points trail off to the right. Nothing jumps out.

Switch to log–log axes and the structure becomes clear. The in-degree distribution stretches across two orders of magnitude with a heavy tail — the signature of a hub network. A handful of heroes vacuum up a disproportionate share of incoming links. The out-degree tail is softer, dying much earlier. Two different shapes in the same network.

03
In vs out: two completely different people.
Cultural weight is not the same as narrative complexity.

In-degree and out-degree select for different things. An in-link is conferred: some other article's editors decided this character was worth mentioning. An out-link is authored: someone sat down and wrote it into this page. One has no ceiling. The other has a budget.

106 Spider-Man's in-degree. 106 of the 302 other heroes reference him. That's 35% of the entire network — referencing one node. His out-degree: 9.

Spider-Man doesn't just have high centrality. He's become a reference point — the "you are here" marker of the Marvel universe on Wikipedia. Editors invoke him as context for characters who never met him.

The out-degree leaders tell a completely different story. Betsy Braddock (Psylocke) links out to 28 heroes; only 7 link back. Her page catalogs decades of team affiliations and retcons across the X-Men, Excalibur, and three different bodies. Adam Warlock, Cloak and Dagger — characters whose Wikipedia pages are essentially annotated relationship maps. They reach out; the network doesn't always reach back.

The asymmetry in one sentence: prominence and connectivity select for completely different things. Cultural weight accumulates passively — the already-famous get mentioned more. Connectivity takes work. Someone has to write the links.

04
Drawing the graph.
277 heroes in one giant connected component.

Convert to undirected and find connected components. 277 of 303 heroes (91%) sit in one giant component. Outside it: 18 smaller pieces — one cluster of 9, and 17 true isolates. 

components = sorted(
    nx.connected_components(G.to_undirected()),
    key=len, reverse=True
)
print(len(components[0]))   # 277
print(len(components) - 1)  # 18 other components

Some of these are genuinely obscure — 1940s one-appearance anthology characters. But others are not. Miracleman has a rich publication history but a legally contested Wikipedia presence, carefully kept separate from mainstream Marvel continuity. Yo-Yo Rodriguez (from the Avengers Initiative) and Baymax (Big Hero 6) have real fan bases — their isolation is a Wikipedia editing artifact, not a measure of cultural weight.

The islands are a map of Marvel's forgotten corners and Wikipedia's uneven edit history. They're not noise. They're the part of the dataset the degree distribution can't tell you about.

What surprised us

The gap between Spider-Man and everyone else is larger than we expected. In-degree 106 versus Hulk's 64 versus Wolverine's 60 — that's not a small lead, that's a different order of magnitude of cultural saturation. Spider-Man has become a unit of measurement inside Marvel's Wikipedia ecosystem.

We also didn't expect 17 true isolates. The course note says "17 characters have no edge in either direction" and we expected them to be pure noise — but listing them out, several have decades of publication history. The network reflects how Wikipedia was written, not just what Marvel published. That gap is worth remembering every time we interpret a result from this dataset.
