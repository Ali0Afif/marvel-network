Spider-Man is referenced by 35% of all Marvel Wikipedia pages. He links back to 9 of them. The gap between those two numbers is the whole story.

HALL  I

THE UNIVERSE

303 nodes · 1,784 directed edges

Every node is a character from Wikipedia's Category: Marvel Comics superheroes. Every directed edge is a Wikipedia link — page A links to page B. 303 heroes. 1,784 arrows. One frozen snapshot.

The critical engineering detail: add nodes before edges. Otherwise NetworkX silently drops the 17 heroes whose pages carry no links at all. Adding the full node list first keeps them in the graph — absent from the edge list, but present in the universe where they belong.

import networkx as nx, pandas as pd

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#")
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    header=None, names=["source","target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes["node_id"])      # isolates survive
G.add_edges_from(zip(edges["source"], edges["target"]))
# → 303 nodes · 1784 directed edges
3% Network density. Only 3 in 100 possible connections exist. 97% of hero pairs never mention each other. Average degree: 9.5. A sparse, real-world network — exactly what network science is designed to study.

Collapse to undirected: 1,784 arrows become 1,434 unique pairs — 350 edges point both ways. The rest are one-way streets. A directed link is an editorial act: someone chose to write that sentence.

On linear axes the shape looks unremarkable — a steep pile near zero, a flat tail. Switch to log–log and the structure becomes unmissable: a heavy tail reaching Spider-Man at k = 106. The out-degree tail dies at k = 28 (Betsy Braddock). Same network. Two very different shapes.

HALL  III

THE TWO FACES

In-degree vs out-degree — different people

106 Spider-Man's in-degree. 106 of 302 possible heroes reference him — 35% of the entire network pointing to one node. His out-degree: 9. The gap is not a quirk. It is the whole phenomenon.

An in-link is conferred: another article's editors decided this character was worth mentioning. An out-link is authored: someone sat down and wrote it into this page. One has no ceiling. The other has a budget. Two different generating mechanisms, two completely different distributions.

Spider-Man doesn't just have high centrality — he's become a reference point. Editors invoke him as context for characters who never met him. He is the network's "you are here" sign.

The connectors — Betsy Braddock, Cloak and Dagger, Adam Warlock — have modest fame but rich story histories that pull them into contact with many corners of the Marvel universe. Their Wikipedia pages reflect that: many mentions of other heroes, few mentions by others. They are the ones doing the linking, not the ones being linked to.

HALL  IV

THE OBSERVATORY

277 heroes in one giant component

91% Giant component coverage. 277 of 303 heroes form one connected giant component. The rest fragment into one cluster of 9 and 17 true isolates. The Marvel universe is almost entirely one conversation.

The giant component is a sign of a real social network: connectivity concentrates. Remove Spider-Man, Hulk, and Wolverine — the three biggest hubs — and the component barely fractures. The network has redundant paths. It is resilient.

HALL  V

HALL OF THE LOST

17 heroes the graph forgot

17 True isolates. Heroes with no incoming and no outgoing links in the Wikipedia network. Present in the node list. Absent from every edge. A Wikipedia editing artifact, not a measure of cultural weight.

These are not obscure characters. Baymax anchors a Disney franchise. Miracleman has a legally contested but celebrated publication history. Yo-Yo Rodriguez appeared in Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. Their isolation reflects how Wikipedia was written, not what Marvel published.

END OF EXHIBITION I

What surprised us

The gap between Spider-Man and everyone else is larger than we expected. In-degree 106 vs Hulk's 64 — that's not a small lead, it's a different category of node. And we didn't expect 17 true isolates, several with decades of publication history. The network reflects how Wikipedia was written, not the Marvel universe itself. That gap matters every time we interpret a result from this dataset.
