Every node is a character from Wikipedia's Category: Marvel Comics superheroes. Every directed edge is a link: page A mentions page B. 303 heroes. 1,784 arrows. The critical detail: add nodes before edges, or NetworkX silently drops the 17 heroes with no links.

FINDING 01

THE MARVEL UNIVERSE IS NOT ONE PERFECTLY CONNECTED WORLD.

Collapse to undirected: 1,784 arrows become 1,434 unique pairs — 350 edges point both ways. Network density: 3%. 97% of possible connections don't exist. Average degree: 9.5. A sparse network — exactly what network science is built to study.

The giant component contains 277 heroes (91%). There's also a cluster of 9, and 17 true isolates — heroes whose Wikipedia pages carry no links at all, in either direction.

What surprised us

The network reflects how Wikipedia was written, not the Marvel universe itself. Several isolates — Baymax, Miracleman, Yo-Yo Rodriguez — have rich publication histories. They are absent from the graph because of how Wikipedia's editors chose to write their pages, not because they are unimportant in the comics.

That gap — between the editorial network and the creative universe — matters every time we interpret a result from this dataset. The network is a record of attention, not of story.
