Are your friends more popular than you? The mathematics of the friendship paradox, applied to 303 Marvel heroes.

↓ scroll to discover

Imagine you are a Marvel superhero. You know a number of other characters — some by alliance, some by rivalry, all by the network of Wikipedia links. Now here is a strange question:

THE PARADOX

ON AVERAGE, YOUR CONNECTIONS ARE MORE CONNECTED THAN YOU.

This is the friendship paradox — a mathematical property of almost every real network. It occurs because hubs (highly connected nodes) appear in many people's friend lists, pulling the average up. Spider-Man is everyone's neighbor. That skews the math.

Below, pick any Marvel character. We will calculate their in-degree — how popular they are — and compare it against the average popularity of everyone they connect with. Most of the time, their connections will win.

MOST POPULAR CONNECTIONS

Who are the "popular friends"?

The characters who most strongly drive the paradox are the hubs — the Spider-Mans and Hulks who appear in many connections' lists. Whenever they are your neighbor, they pull your average up. That is why the paradox is not a coincidence: it is a structural property of any network where degree distribution is skewed.

FINDING 03

IS THERE A SUPERHERO NOBODY OUT-POPULARS?

Yes — and it is Spider-Man. With 106 incoming links, the probability that any character has more popular connections than Spider-Man himself approaches zero. He is not just a hub. He is the paradox personified.

The friendship paradox holds for the vast majority of Marvel heroes. The rare exceptions — heroes whose connections are less connected than themselves — are the ones who link primarily to each other and to isolated characters, not to the great hubs. Find them with the selector above.
